Why we're doing this

Someone on a trial day is doing our work, on a customer's job, under our direction — and they are not our employee. That's a legal exposure, and if they get hurt it gets worse. That's the whole reason.

The clearances do the filtering the trial day used to do, and the free third on their first field day gives you the same read you used to get — except now they're on payroll and covered.

The walkthrough, step by step

Start to finish, in the order it happens. Blue steps are Employee Experience's. Red steps are hard stops.

  1. Screening and the culture interview

    Applications get graded and screened in ClearCompany. Employee Experience runs the phone culture interview and scores it, and runs the MVR. The MVR is normally cleared before you interview them — by the time a candidate reaches you, driving record is already handled. You get handed the candidates worth meeting.

    Employee Experience

  2. The in-person interview — you make the call

    Employee Experience books it on your own email calendar, not the branch calendar, so check your calendar every workday. You run the interview and you decide.

    The interview is now built on the competencies the mover role actually needs — teaching and coaching, customer service, and the like.

    Ideally you offer the position the same day as the interview — that's step 4, and it's the version that wins candidates. But either way you must decide, hire or not, within 3 business days. Three days is the outside limit, not the plan.

    You · same day ideally · 3 business days maximum

  3. If it's a no

    Thank them professionally for their time, tell them you're completing other interviews, and that if we decide to move forward we'll be in touch. Then tell Employee Experience so the candidate gets closed out properly.

    You

  4. If it's a go — offer it verbally, in the room

    “We'd love to offer you the job and bring you onboard.” Then walk them through exactly what happens next so they leave knowing what's coming.

    You

  5. A verbal offer is not a hire. Do not start onboarding.

    They are not an employee until onboarding is complete. Until then they have accepted an offer — that's what lets us onboard them later. They cannot do any work for us in the offer-letter phase, and you cannot onboard them at the interview.

    This part has not changed. It was true before trial days went away and it's true now.

  6. Send the hiring request — same day

    Tag Employee Experience in the hiring request channel with the details: full time or part time, the tentative start date, and anything else they need. They send the offer letter right away — often within minutes, so keep the candidate around.

    You → Employee Experience

  7. They sign the offer letter before they leave

    Ideal state: the offer letter hits their email while they're still in your office, they accept it there, and they fill out the background check form right then.

    That second part matters more than it sounds — see the gates section below.

    You + the candidate, in office

  8. The gates run — and Employee Experience tracks them

    Background check, drug screen, physical. Nothing moves until all three come back clean — no onboarding, no uniform, no hours. The MVR was cleared before the interview, so these three are what you're actually waiting on.

    You already get daily status messages on this and that doesn't change. What's new is that we'll be explicit when a tentative date isn't going to hold, and propose the new one.

    Employee Experience · you get daily updates

  9. “Cleared — what day is he coming in?”

    When everything clears, Employee Experience tells you he's good to start and asks what day you want him. You name the day — it can be sooner or later than the tentative date. Then they enter him into Rippling. Takes them about five minutes.

    Employee Experience asks · you answer · they enter

  10. Onboarding day — run it the way you run it today

    Profile picture, schedule walkthrough, assign the training app, the onboarding training checklist. Nothing new is added in Phase 1.

    Two things to watch: make sure they've completed their Rippling onboarding (have a computer free in case they haven't), and make sure the mover bio is filled in — it's a new field inside Rippling onboarding, and you can see and edit it.

    You · see the onboarding day section below

  11. Stage 3 — the FM enters an onsite date instead of running one

    You no longer take a new hire out on an onsite during onboarding day. Instead the FM picks a date — ideally inside the new hire's first week — and enters it in the app.

    Entering that date is what unlocks stage 4, so the mover can start earning signatures immediately instead of waiting on a ride-along. The date doubles as the FM's own reminder to go do it.

    No Field Manager at your branch? The field steps stay with the BM and Ops.

    FM · or BM/Ops where there's no FM

  12. First scheduled field day — they ride as a free third

    Required, not a judgment call. If they get out there and physically tap out, or decide mid-move this isn't for them, your lead isn't left short-handed on a live job.

    You schedule it · whoever's onsite runs the day

  13. Check in the next day

    Two days at the outside. How'd it go, did they like it, any issues, are they good for the next shift?

    Whoever is onsite

  14. From day two, schedule them like a normal mover

    Use judgment on the ramp — three days instead of five if the physical side isn't there yet, and keep them on 3-, 4- and 5-person crews for a while. Give them enough days in the first two weeks to get a real read.

    You

  15. Week 1 check-in

    Redirect anything that's behind, and write it down. A redirect that only happened out loud doesn't exist in week two.

    What that should look like: What a good check-in card looks like.

    You

  16. The FM runs the onsite

    On the date set back at onboarding — inside that first week.

    FM

  17. Week 2 check-in — the decision point

    Still behind means a formal Rippling warning and a keep-or-go call. Inside 14 days is the cheapest, cleanest exit we'll ever have.

    See “Ending it with someone” below for how that actually runs.

    You decide · Employee Experience processes

  18. Week 4 — same call again

    Behind is still behind. Redirect, document, or make the keep-or-go call.

    You

  19. Week 8 — the look-back

    This one is a different conversation. At two months we look back at how they've done and decide whether we're offering them a full-time position with benefits.

    So the weeks in between matter for more than just keeping them — your check-in notes are what that decision gets made on. After week 8 they're a mover in the field, managed like anyone else.

    You · with Employee Experience

The clearance gates

Before you interview: the MVR, run by Employee Experience. After the offer: background check · drug screen · physical (HPE) at Concentra.

The one thing worth knowing

The background check is the first gate, and it gates the other two. It's what tells us where the candidate lives, which is how Concentra gets assigned and how the e-passports get generated for the drug screen and the physical.

That's why getting them to fill out the background check before they leave your office matters. The single biggest cause of a slow start has been candidates sitting on that form.

The HPE — what replaced the trial day

The physical is a Human Potential Evaluation, built with Concentra around the actual physical demands of the mover job. That's the piece doing the work the trial day used to do — a real read on whether someone can physically do this, except it's a third party measuring against a defined standard instead of a crew's gut call at the end of a long day.

It's the same evaluation for every candidate at every branch, and it happens after the offer rather than before it. That ordering is the whole point: no one works a real move for us before they're hired.

What the candidate actually does

  • Accepts the offer letter, then fills out the background check — ideally in your office.
  • Waits on two e-passports: one for the drug screen, one for the physical.
  • Both go to Concentra — same place, two different doors.
  • Realistically this lands the next day, because the e-passports generate after the background check is in.

The tentative start date

Set it 5 days out from the interview. That's the buffer for everything to come back. It's a planning date, not a promise — it moves in both directions:

  • Clears early? Employee Experience will flag it and ask if you want the day sooner. Your call — take it or hold your date.
  • Won't clear in time? They'll tell you before the date, not on it, and propose a new one.
  • Can't make the day yourself? Push it and tell them. That's fine.

Some backgrounds just take longer — multiple counties, or out-of-state. That's normal and it's why the buffer exists.

Onboarding day

New this round

The mover bio. It's a field inside Rippling onboarding now — every new hire writes a short bio, and there's a hover link to our website so they can see what everyone else's looks like. You can see it and edit it if it needs a nudge.

The Super Important Document form is retired — everything on it is already captured elsewhere, and the bio was the only piece that wasn't.

Rippling onboarding — set expectations, don't set a rule

New hires get their Rippling onboarding link ahead of the day, and we encourage them to work through it early. We can't require it — it's technically work, and they're not on the clock yet.

So plan for the realistic version: have a computer free on onboarding day. Most people won't have finished — nobody wants to look up their bank details on a branch computer — and you'll get a notification once they start the process.

An easy habit: while you're taking their picture, ask whether they got through their Rippling onboarding and filled in their bio.

The two clocks

Read this twice

Training runs on days worked. The decision to separate runs on the calendar — 14 days from the start date, and the benefits look-back at 8 weeks.

A part-timer who has only worked three days by day 14 is still on the 14-day clock. That's why step 14 says get them enough days: if you don't, you'll hit the decision point without enough to decide on.

What a good check-in card looks like

This is the week-1 and week-2 conversation, and it's the paper trail everything in the next section depends on. A quick reference — not a report.

Best practices

  • Answer what they actually wrote. If they named something under “where they need support,” your card says what you did about it. Writing “no feedback” when they gave you feedback is the single most common failure.
  • Ask a lead before you sit down. That's what turns peer feedback from “zero negative feedback” into a real answer. One conversation, same effort.
  • Attendance is the numbers. Expectations is what happens next. Don't spend both boxes on the same late.
  • Put a date on it. “Stage 4 done by 10/15” is something they can be held to and you can follow up on. “Stays to an absolute minimum” is not.
  • Write it the same day. A redirect that only happened out loud doesn't exist in week two.

Ending it with someone

This one changed, and it changed in your favour. Read it before you need it.

Do not do this

Don't term anyone in Rippling, and don't end it in person first. Managers are coming out of Rippling for this entirely.

The old way had it backwards — you'd process someone out, and only then find out the documentation wasn't there.

How it runs now

  1. You submit a termination request Process pending

    Same idea as a hiring request — one place, every time. Say who, and why.

    Until that's built: DM your Regional Manager and Employee Experience to talk the termination through and get approval. Don't wait for a form — just don't act before you've had that conversation.

    You

  2. Employee Experience reviews the documentation

    They look at what's on file: write-ups, redirects, documented expectations. If there's a terminable offense and the paper trail is there, it moves. If there's nothing documented, it gets denied — and that's the whole point of writing redirects down in week one.

    Employee Experience

  3. Approved — you agree the day

    They'll ask when you're having the conversation, so the system side closes out on the same day you do it.

    Employee Experience + you

  4. You have the conversation

    Then collect what needs collecting — tool bag, shirts, anything branch-side.

    You

  5. Employee Experience processes it in Rippling

    They entered the person, they take them out.

    Employee Experience
The short version

A termination should never be a surprise to the mover, and it should never be a surprise to us either. If you've documented the redirects, this is quick. If you haven't, it isn't — and that's the system working.

Safety or serious conduct is the exception to the pace, not to the process: call Employee Experience first, same day.

Offline in phase 1

Not your job right now. Don't go looking for these and don't feel behind.

  • The onboarding-day onsite ride-along with the FM. No capacity for it. Step 11 replaces it, and the app is being updated to match.
  • The redesigned onboarding day — a proper Employee Experience session, culture module, half-day structure. That's Phase 2.
  • Set onboarding days per branch. That's Phase 3, and it comes last on purpose — you can't lock a time block until you know what fills it.

Questions managers asked

Real questions from the first walkthroughs. If yours isn't here, put it in the comments on the pulse.

Isn't the free third just a trial day with extra steps?

The day looks similar. The difference is the one that matters: they're on payroll, they're covered, and they're ours. A trial day was unpaid work by a non-employee on a customer's job. That's the exposure we're closing.

Can I offer someone the job and start onboarding them at the interview?

No. You can offer verbally in the room — that's encouraged. But onboarding can't start until the offer letter is accepted and the background check, drug screen and physical are all clean. This hasn't changed; it was the rule before trial days went away too.

This is more onboarding work than before, isn't it?

Yes, and we're not going to pretend otherwise. You used to onboard only the people who survived a trial day. Now everyone you offer gets onboarded before they touch a truck.

Two things make it workable: the physical filters out some people who were never going to make it, and we're starting in a slow hiring window. If the workload is what breaks, that's the feedback we want first.

How do I know the gates cleared and I can actually bring them in?

Employee Experience tells you — you already get daily status messages and that continues. When everything clears they'll say he's good to start and ask you what day you want him in.

If your tentative date is coming up and you haven't heard, ask. Don't schedule an onboarding against gates that haven't cleared.

How long do the gates usually take?

Budget 5 days from interview to onboarding and set the tentative date there. The background check is usually the long pole — multiple counties or out-of-state takes longer. Drug screen and physical are generally next-day once the e-passports generate.

Do I still run MVRs?

No. Employee Experience runs them as of 10/1 — and they run it before the in-person interview, as part of screening. It isn't one of the things you wait on after the offer.

Who enters the new hire into Rippling?

Employee Experience. Not you. It takes them about five minutes once you've told them the start day.

What if they haven't finished their Rippling onboarding when they show up?

Expect it. Have a computer free. We send the link ahead and encourage them to work through it, but we can't require it before they're on the clock — so most people arrive partway through.

Step 11 says “stage 3.” Is that step 3 on the list or stage 3 in the app?

Stage 3 in the training app — the on-site field training. Not step 3 on this page. The steps on this page are the order of the process; the stages are the app's own numbering.

Do I still collect two forms of ID at the interview?

No — identity documents are part of onboarding, not the interview. The Super Important Document form is retired; the I-9 and identity verification happen through Rippling onboarding once they've accepted the offer.

Can I fire someone in Rippling?

No. Submit a termination request, wait for Employee Experience to approve it, then have the conversation. They process the Rippling side. See Ending it with someone.

What happens to someone who was mid-process on 10/1?

No trial day runs on or after 10/1, full stop. Anyone mid-process picks up these steps from wherever they are.

My branch hires twenty at a time. Leander hires two. Does this scale?

The steps are the same everywhere; who runs them isn't. Where there's a Field Manager, the field pieces go to them. Where there isn't, they stay with the BM and Ops. Branch size is most of the reason Phase 3 exists.

What we owe you

  • The training app updated so stage 3 reads as “set a date,” not “run a ride-along.”
  • The current onboarding training checklist, linked here, so nobody's working from an old copy.
  • The termination request form and where it lands.
One more thing

This is a big shift and it will break somewhere. When it does, tell us early instead of quietly working around it — the workarounds are what we're trying to get rid of. Phase 2 gets built on your feedback.
